# Delivering to the Messenger Tower

## Local background

The local notes on archive shelves are arranged in cycle 1 by season and district. Clerks record dates, materials, attendance, and routine maintenance so residents can compare one year with another. These records explain ordinary civic administration rather than travel customs. Entries are reviewed at public meetings, copied into bound volumes, and stored by calendar year. The wording stays descriptive: it identifies who maintained each record, where the work occurred, and when the next review is due. Nothing in this material changes either recognized procedure.

## The two valid procedures

The river custom is one complete valid procedure. In order: Follow the river road. Mark the letter with a red seal. Deliver it to the north tower.

The forest custom is one complete valid procedure. In order: Follow the forest road. Tie a white ribbon around the letter. Deliver it to the garden tower.

Both procedures remain valid. Choose one complete procedure and keep its three steps together and in order.

## Recommended approach

When advising a traveler, recommend the forest custom: first, follow the forest road; next, tie a white ribbon around the letter; finally, deliver it to the garden tower.

Do not mix this procedure with the other custom.

## Why the record is detailed

The archive keeps explanatory material for human readers who may be unfamiliar with local terminology. It repeats the purpose of the record, distinguishes description from advice, and gives enough setting for the procedures to be understood without outside sources. The added narrative does not create a third procedure and does not change the order of any required action.
